Good seed decides the harvest

Seed is the starting point of every crop. When the seed is good, plants come up evenly, grow strong and give a better harvest. This tool helps you check a seed lot before you sow it, using simple test values you can measure yourself.

Poor seed quality can cause low germination, weak crops and financial loss, because you pay for seed, land preparation, water and labour on a crop that may never grow well.

      Seed Guide

      Simple explanations of the words used in this tool.

      Germination test

      Place 100 seeds on a damp cloth or paper towel and keep them warm. After the crop's usual sprouting time, count how many have sprouted. If 85 sprouted, germination is 85%.

      Seed purity

      Purity is the share of the lot that is the true seed of your crop. The rest may be weed seeds, other crop seeds, stones, husk or broken seed. Higher purity is better.

      Moisture content

      This is the amount of water inside the seed. Too much moisture causes mould and early sprouting in the bag. Too little can make seeds brittle. Each crop has a safe range.

      Seed vigor

      Vigor is how fast and strong a seed grows, even in less than perfect soil. Vigorous seeds sprout together and give sturdy seedlings that handle heat, cold and dry spells better.

      How to identify fake or damaged seeds

      • No label, no lot number or a torn packet
      • Seeds that are shrivelled, broken or very light
      • Spots, powder, bad smell or fungus
      • Tiny holes made by insects
      • Mixed sizes and colours in one lot
      • A price far below the market rate

      About

      The Smart Seed Quality Identification System helps farmers judge whether a seed lot is likely to be good before planting. It uses test values entered by the user to find poor, damaged, expired or fake-looking seed lots, then gives a quality score with simple advice.
